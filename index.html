ABOUT SITEIQ PRO

What SiteIQ does, and who it is for

SiteIQ is a free preliminary site-selection and feasibility screen for architects, developers and city planners. It finds candidate parcels from public data, checks what could physically fit, sketches a conceptual building, prices it with a transparent pro forma and exports drawings and reports. It is a first look before due diligence — not a replacement for it.

Two ways to start

  • Find sites in a city — SiteIQ ranks the city’s strongest neighborhoods, pulls vacant parcels from public county or state GIS, screens out lots with buildings, roads, ponds or no street access, and lists the best that pass.
  • Analyze an address — the same checks and model for one parcel you already have in mind.

Finding more sites

  • + Find more sites searches the next neighborhoods (then deeper into ones that still have unreviewed lots) with the same filters and ranking, and shows only parcels you haven’t been shown. Each batch is ranked on its own, best first.
  • SiteIQ remembers which parcels you’ve reviewed in each city (by county parcel ID, in this browser only), so searching the same city again also skips them. View all brings back every site from this session; Reset search history starts over and never touches saved analyses.
  • When a city’s qualifying parcels run out, SiteIQ says so instead of repeating old sites. Address lookup always returns the exact parcel, seen or not.

Reading a site

  • Snapshot — pick a site on the map or in the list for a quick summary: screening result, buildable envelope, potential building, estimated cost, financial status and the top three things to check.
  • Open full analysis opens a wide workspace with five sections: Overview (is it worth pursuing, risks, data confidence, next steps), Site & Massing (site plan, 3D massing, floors and zoning), Financial Analysis (summary, budget, revenue & operations, financing, returns, and Advanced: what-if, Monte Carlo, sensitivity), Market & Context (walkability, transit, income, trends, flood, soil, utilities) and Reports & Exports. ← Map & results (or Esc) returns to the map with everything kept.
  • Three separate conclusions. Every site shows Physical feasibility (lot geometry, frontage, setbacks, slope, flood, soils), Regulatory feasibility (zoning — always inferred from the district type, never verified against the ordinance; where no zoning layer exists: “Zoning not verified — conceptual building envelope only.”) and Financial feasibility (the modeled economics). Passing one never implies the others.
  • Status labels, used the same way on screen and in every export: Source record (retrieved from a named public dataset during this analysis — not field-verified), Modeled estimate (calculated from assumptions or reference data), Requires verification (confirm with an official source or a qualified professional), Unavailable (SiteIQ could not obtain it reliably).
  • Data coverage is set per site from the sources that actually answered: Enhanced (public parcel geometry and a local zoning code), Standard (public parcel geometry, no zoning layer) or Limited (no legal parcel boundary — lot, envelope and building are approximate). The ? buttons explain finance terms; Assumptions (gear) changes filters, massing rules, scoring weights and every finance assumption.

What SiteIQ is — and isn’t

It is a fast, transparent first screen using free public data. It is not a zoning determination, survey, title search, environmental assessment, appraisal or lender quote. Before acting on any site, confirm zoning with the local authority and engage licensed professionals.

SiteIQ Pro · free public beta · build 2026-10-17 · no account, no tracking, no paid features.

SiteIQ Pro treats site selection as a multi-criteria decision problem. It finds candidate lots, checks that each can be built on, scores the lots on six categories, and runs a financial model on each one.

1. Finding real, buildable lots

Parcels come from public state or county GIS layers. If a state has no statewide layer, the tool looks for a county layer in the ArcGIS Online public catalog, and falls back to OpenStreetMap only if nothing else is available. A lot is kept only if it meets all of these:

  • It has no building footprint on it (FEMA USA Structures, or CAGIS in Hamilton County).
  • It touches a public street (Census TIGER roads).
  • It is large enough for the chosen building after zoning setbacks.
  • It is not a park, cemetery, right-of-way or utility parcel.

Next-door vacant lots that share an edge are tested as a combined “assembly.”

2. What can be built (massing)

The zoning code is matched to a zoning family (single-family, multi-family, mixed-use, commercial or industrial). Each family sets typical setbacks, a height limit, a floor-area ratio and lot coverage. Floors, units and parking (surface, structured or none) come from the envelope that fits inside those limits. Retail goes on the ground floor only.

3. Scoring: TOPSIS with AHP weights

Each site is scored on six categories:

  • Buildability: flood zone, slope and soil.
  • Infrastructure: hydrants, transit and roads.
  • Sustainability: solar and air quality.
  • Accessibility: EPA walkability.
  • Market: income and amenities.
  • Risk.

The weights come from the Analytic Hierarchy Process (Saaty) and can be changed in Assumptions. Sites are ranked by TOPSIS, which measures how close each one is to an ideal site and how far it is from the worst one (Hwang and Yoon).

4. Pro forma and Monte Carlo

Each site gets a preliminary month-by-month development model, the way lenders and developers screen a ground-up project. Every number below is the value currently in use; change them under Assumptions → Finance → Advanced.

  • Best-fit building: every height from 2 floors up to the zoning (or your) maximum is tested, and the one with the largest development spread is shown. 2–3 floors are wood walk-ups (no elevator), 4–5 floors add an elevator, taller buildings use podium or concrete construction.
  • What to build: each lot is also tested as apartments, a 2–4 unit building and rental townhomes (garages, at most 24 units per acre). Options zoning appears to prohibit are flagged as hypothetical.
  • Timeline: land closes in month 0; construction takes 9–36 months depending on height and size; lease-up runs at new leases a month (at least 6 months with retail); the sale happens after completion.
  • Land: three separate figures. The official county land value (the tax roll, shown as published). The model-estimated acquisition price = county land value × (Advanced), or, where no county value exists, a location-based estimate from walkability, retail activity, county income and state price level (low confidence). Your purchase price, entered at the top of Financial Analysis, always takes precedence. SiteIQ never uses listings, sales or comparables, and never presents an estimate as a market price.
  • Budget: the building hard cost — your $/sf × a regional price adjustment (BEA Regional Price Parities; RPP is a general consumer price index, not a construction-cost index, so it is only a rough proxy you can turn down or off) × a height factor, or your own $/gross sf entered at the top of Financial Analysis — covers the structure, envelope, interiors and MEP only. Separate lines: parking, elevators, sitework (/sf of lot), utility connections and tap fees ( per unit), paving removal on surface lots (/sf; demolishing an existing building is not estimated), a contingency, architecture & engineering , permits & impact fees , other soft costs , construction interest, a loan fee and property tax on the land during construction.
  • Construction loan: up to of total cost at your construction rate; equity goes in first; interest is added to the loan until the building is leased.
  • Operations: rents start from Census median contract rent (tract and county) raised by a new-building premium — an estimate, not a current asking rent. Vacancy residential and retail. Operating costs /unit/yr for elevator buildings and for small walk-ups (regionally scaled), management ( under 20 units), reserves /unit, other income /unit/month. Property tax uses an approximate state rate for rental property. Retail is triple-net with non-recoverable costs, /sf tenant buildout and leasing commissions. Operating costs are split into insurance (22%), maintenance & repairs (33%), utilities (27%) and administrative (18%). Each year is built from these line items: rents and other income grow a year; operating costs, reserves and property tax grow , independently; management and retail non-recoverables follow collected income.
  • Permanent loan: at stabilization the construction loan is refinanced at your permanent rate ( amortization after of interest-only payments, fee). Payments follow a month-by-month schedule: the final payment is only what is owed, payments stop once the loan is repaid, and the sale pays off the actual remaining balance (never below zero). The loan is the smallest of of value, the amount the income covers , and a debt yield. A shortfall versus the construction loan is an additional equity call. The Finance tab separates initial equity, additional equity (overruns above the loan limit, lease-up losses, refinance shortfall) and peak equity.
  • Value and sale: stabilized value uses a cap rate below your exit cap; the sale uses the exit cap on the next year's NOI, less selling costs.
  • Returns: levered IRR, MIRR and NPV on the monthly equity cash flows; equity profit, equity multiple, year-1 cash-on-cash (on peak equity) and break-even rent (first-year NOI = debt service); yield on cost, DSCR and debt yield. The IRR is solved so that NPV at that rate is zero (checked every time); when the cash flows have no IRR or more than one, SiteIQ says so and shows MIRR instead of a number. Development spread (value − cost) is a paper margin, not realized profit. The site score measures the parcel and location only; the economics are reported separately.

A 1,000-run Monte Carlo is centred on your current assumptions and varies: hard cost ×exp(N(3%, 8%)) (overruns more likely than savings); residential rent ±7% σ and exit cap ±50 bp σ (correlated −0.4); retail rent ±9% σ; vacancy ±2 points σ; rent growth ±1 point σ; expense growth ±0.75 point σ; land ×exp(N(0, 10%)); interest rates ±75 bp σ; a 35% chance of a 1–6 month delay; lease-up pace ×exp(N(0, 25%)). Its percentages are model outputs under those assumptions, not real-world odds. A sensitivity chart shows which assumption moves the IRR most. All outputs are estimates for early screening only.

5. Fairness

Walkability and income are tied to past investment, so scores that use them can steer development toward areas that already have it. The optional equity lens and fairness constraint correct for that by giving extra weight to below-median-income tracts and checking that the top results are not concentrated in high-income areas.

Known limits

  • Lot lines come from GIS, not a survey.
  • Vacancy is judged from building footprints, which can be a few years old.
  • The zoning rules are typical values for each zoning family, not the local code text.
  • Utility capacity, title, easements, environmental contamination and entitlement risk are not modeled.

All data is fetched live from public, keyless services when you run a search. Nothing is sent to a SiteIQ server; the app has none. Recent responses are cached in your browser only.

Confirm before any real decision

  • Zoning: permitted uses, height, FAR, setbacks, parking, overlays, transitions and variances — with the local zoning office. SiteIQ infers zoning from the district type only.
  • Property: ownership, availability, title, easements and the exact boundary — with the county auditor/recorder and an ALTA survey. Lot lines come from public GIS, not a survey.
  • Site conditions: geotechnical (soil maps are not borings), environmental history (Phase I ESA), floodplain elevation, utilities capacity and tap fees.
  • Costs and income: contractor pricing, market rents and comparables, lender terms and appraised value. Construction cost is a preliminary $/gross sf assumption; rents start from Census medians; land starts from the county value.
  • Existing conditions: building footprints can be several years old — check Street View or visit the site.

Geographic coverage

Public parcel and zoning data differ by state and county. Hamilton County, Ohio (Cincinnati) has county parcels, zoning, overlays and building footprints. Elsewhere SiteIQ searches state and county layers in the public ArcGIS catalog; some counties have parcels but no zoning layer, and some have neither. Results never claim the same capability everywhere: every site carries an Enhanced, Standard or Limited coverage label and says what could not be checked.

Free public services

SiteIQ relies on free public APIs (FEMA, Census, EPA, USDA, USGS, OpenStreetMap Nominatim and Overpass, Open-Meteo, NASA POWER, county ArcGIS servers). Requests are rate-limited per service, de-duplicated, cached in your browser, time out after 8–40 seconds and are retried at most once. When a service is down, the affected item is labeled Unavailable — never filled with a made-up value. Nominatim allows about one request per second and is not meant for heavy traffic; a high-traffic public deployment should use its own geocoder or a hosted plan (not included, to keep SiteIQ free).

Privacy

  • No account, no server, no analytics, no cookies, no advertising. SiteIQ is a single web page; nothing you do is sent to its author.
  • What leaves your browser: the city or address you search and the coordinates of candidate lots go directly to the public services above to fetch data. Their own privacy policies apply.
  • What stays in your browser: saved analyses, recent searches, reviewed-parcel history per city (parcel IDs only), your theme and assumptions (local storage), and cached public-data responses for up to 30 days (IndexedDB). Clear them with Clear cached data, Reset search history, deleting saved analyses, or your browser’s site-data settings.
  • “Report a problem” only copies a text report to your clipboard; it is sent nowhere unless you paste it yourself.

No compliance with a specific privacy regulation is claimed.
